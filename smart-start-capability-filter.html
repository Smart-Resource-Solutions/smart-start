The Capability Filter

A missing skill isn't the same as too much work, and it needs a different answer. Work through these four, roughly in order.

About 10 minutes
Your data stays in this browser
Why this is different from Capacity: Capacity is about having more work than time. Capability is about not having a skill at all, no amount of reshuffling the calendar fixes that. This filter is about closing a genuine skills gap, not a busy week.
1

Audit internal talent

Take stock of the hidden skills, past experience and career interests already sitting inside your team. The capability you need may already be here, just not visible from where you're standing.

Ask yourselfWho has relevant experience from a previous role, a side project, or a qualification that's never actually been used here?
2

Upskill or reskill

If the gap is small to moderate, training, a course, a certification or some structured mentoring can close it. It's usually cheaper than it sounds, and it tends to build loyalty and retention, not just capability.

Ask yourselfIs there someone with the right foundation and appetite to grow into this, given real time and support, not just goodwill?
3

Borrow before you buy

If you need the capability now, a freelancer, contractor or agency gets you there without committing to a permanent salary. It also lets you test whether the capability genuinely earns a permanent place in the business before you commit to one.

Ask yourselfCould this be brought in for one defined piece of work first, rather than as an open ended commitment?
4

Adopt targeted technology

Sometimes the gap isn't really a people gap at all. A specific tool, often an AI driven one, can do the specialist work that would otherwise need a dedicated hire, for example handling data analysis rather than hiring a data analyst.

Ask yourselfIs there a tool already built for exactly this problem, rather than a person needed to do it by hand?
On the order: work through these roughly in sequence, but not rigidly. If there's a genuine deadline and nobody internal has the skill, borrowing the capability in now while you explore training for next time is a perfectly reasonable place to start.

Before you write a job description

Do not jump straight to hiring, or to buying new technology, before you've genuinely checked what you already have or could build. Hiring for a gap that training or a contractor could have closed is usually the most expensive way to solve this.

Where to go from here

If none of the four close the gap and this needs a proper role defined, permanent, contract or freelance, use "What does this role need to achieve?" before writing a job description or a brief.
If step 3 (borrow before you buy) is the route, the Freelancer and Contractor Checklist covers what to get right before you engage someone.
If the answer looks more like redesigning the work or automating part of it, "What's the smartest way to get the work done?" covers those options.
