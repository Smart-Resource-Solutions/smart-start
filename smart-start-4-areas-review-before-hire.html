4 Areas to Review Before You Hire

Feeling permanently at capacity doesn't always mean you need more people. Work through these four before you write a job description.

About 10 minutes
Your data stays in this browser
Why these four, in this order: each one can free up real capacity without adding headcount. Hiring is the most expensive and hardest to reverse option on the list, so it's worth being genuinely sure the other four don't already solve it.
1

Audit your processes

Map out your current workflows. Bottlenecks, redundant steps, or places where work quietly gets stuck often eat more capacity than a busy week does, and eliminating that friction can free up time immediately, without anyone working longer hours.

Go to the Process Audit →
2

Leverage automation

Look for repetitive, manual tasks, data entry, scheduling, invoicing, that could be handled by software instead of a person. Moving someone off admin and onto the work that actually needs them can increase real capacity overnight.

Go to the Task and Decision Audit →
3

Cross-train your team

If work only moves because one specific person happens to be available, that isn't really a capacity problem, it's a single point of failure wearing a capacity costume. Cross-training spreads the ability to do a task across more than one person, so a busy period can be absorbed by whoever has room, not just whoever holds that particular skill.

Ask yourselfWhich tasks currently only move because one specific person is available to do them?
Go to the Roles and Responsibilities Map →
4

Review what your capacity is actually spent on

Sometimes a business feels permanently at capacity because it's chasing low margin, high effort work. Freeing up capacity doesn't always mean creating more of it, sometimes it means being more deliberate about what earns a place on the team's plate at all.

Ask yourselfWhich clients, products or services take up far more effort than they're worth, and what would genuinely happen if you said no to the least profitable of them?

This one is a commercial and pricing question more than a people or team one, worth a proper look with whoever handles your numbers, SMART Start's tools don't cover this area directly.

Before you write a job description

Do not skip straight to hiring because it feels like the fastest fix. It's usually the slowest, most expensive, and hardest to reverse option of the four above.

Your next step

If you've genuinely worked through all four and there's still an ongoing gap that nothing here closes, "What does this role need to achieve?" will help you define it properly before you write anything.
